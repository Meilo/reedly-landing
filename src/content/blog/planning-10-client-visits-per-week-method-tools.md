You've just committed to ten client visits for next week. Monday morning, you feel organized. By Wednesday afternoon, you're scrambling through Post-its trying to remember what you were supposed to prepare for Thursday's prospect. Sound familiar?

Most field sales reps who fail at scaling their visit volume don't fail because they lack energy. They fail because they lack method.

## The 48-hour preparation window

Here's what works: **never prepare a visit the day of**. The reps who consistently hit 10+ visits per week all follow the same rule: everything is ready 48 hours in advance.

That means by end-of-day Monday, your Wednesday visits are bulletproof. Files reviewed, questions written, objection responses ready, directions checked. This isn't about being neurotic, it's about not losing 20 minutes in the parking lot scrolling through your CRM in a panic.

The checklist is simple: company background refreshed, previous interaction notes reviewed, specific goal defined (not "have a good conversation"), three questions prepared, and next-step scenarios mapped out. Five minutes per visit when done properly.

## Batch your admin work

The biggest time killer isn't driving, it's context-switching. Jumping from visit prep to email to post-visit notes destroys your day.

Block 90 minutes every Monday and Thursday morning exclusively for visit preparation. No emails, no calls, just systematic prep for the next batch of visits. Then block 60 minutes each evening for post-visit follow-up: notes, CRM updates, follow-up emails.

**Batching cuts administrative time by 40%** compared to doing it "as you go." You enter a rhythm, your brain stays in the same mode, and you catch details you'd otherwise miss.

## Route like you mean it

Ten visits per week means two per day on average. Geography isn't optional anymore, it's your constraint number one. Group visits by zone, not by priority. A slightly less strategic visit that's on the route beats a perfect meeting that costs you two hours of windshield time.

Use Sunday evening or Monday morning to map the week. Clustering visits within a 20-kilometer radius can save you five hours of drive time weekly. That's one full extra visit, or the mental space to show up fresh instead of exhausted.

The reps doing 10+ quality visits aren't superhuman. They've just stopped winging it and started treating preparation as part of the job, not the thing that gets in the way of it.
